The problem

Keep the promotion. Remove the tricks around it.

Temu's coupon event turned a $51 bean bag chair into a $151 checkout, across 16 screens of prize wheels, fake rewards and blocked exits. I kept the real offer (spend $100, get $20 off + 1 gift) and rebuilt everything around it on three principles:

  • Item first: the user can always buy what they came for
  • Rules visible: real prices, running total, no fine print
  • Exit everywhere: same spot, every screen, no guilt

Scroll — the phone on the right follows along →

Entry

Product page: buy what you came for

  • The offer is a quiet, optional note. Tapping it opens the coupon
  • After "Add to cart", the coupon appears once
  • "Buy now" goes straight to checkout, with no event screens in between

Pop-up

Fix the pop-up, don't kill it

  • Shows once, after add to cart, never when the user tries to leave
  • Nothing preselected; the ticket is selected only when tapped
  • "No time limit · optional" replaces the countdown
  • "No thanks, just my item" carries equal weight to "Claim"
  • ×, tap outside or Esc closes it, and it stays closed

The business still gets exposure; the user keeps their autonomy.

Step 1 of 2

Rules first, then pick items

  • Full rules at the top, at body-text size; the 20-day check-in is off by default
  • On scroll, rules collapse to one line you can tap to reopen
  • Real prices, ratings and reviews on every card
  • Pinned footer: "You pay so far" incl. tax, distance to $100, and a skip button

Details

Every item can be inspected

  • Real price, reviews, delivery dates and return policy
  • One button to select or remove, then back to the same spot in the feed

Step 2 of 2

What you pay, what you get

  • Split into: you pay / your gift / you receive / not included
  • The gift unlocks at $100 and shows its real value
  • Short of $100? It says so plainly; the coupon isn't silently dropped
  • No preselected payment method

Done

An honest confirmation

  • Delivery date for every item and gift
  • A single "Keep shopping" button, with no "reward expired" bait
  • If you joined check-in, it tells you where to leave

From my own experience: receiving things I didn't remember buying, and that were hard to return.

Branch

Leaving is one tap

  • × on any step lands here, with no retention pop-up
  • One neutral line of copy; the cart is kept
  • The offer stays available as step 2 of checkout

Outcome

Same offer, honest flow

"I didn't remove the promotion. I removed the tricks around it, and moved the offer to checkout, where purchase intent is highest." Success would be measured by completed orders, refund rate and 30-day repeat purchases, not order value alone.
