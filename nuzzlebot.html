✳NuzzleBot: pet it, and it pets back

A furry little robot that wanders around your home. It can tell how you touch it: stroke it and it’s happy, press too hard and it huffs off. Warm company for people who can’t keep a pet.

Context

From a screaming loaf of bread to a shy cat.

My first PComp piece at ITP was a loaf of bread that screams when you press it. Funny, but I wanted to make something people could feel for.

The spark was my cousin’s cat: it ignores you most of the time, until you scratch the right spot and it squints with joy. Plenty of older adults, kids and young renters want that kind of company but can’t keep a pet.

How might we

How might a small robot show a personality of its own, just from the way it’s touched?

Character

Shy, but very pettable.

We looked at Moflin and LOVOT and settled on a kitten-like character: it wanders off to find its own fun, reacts when you touch it, and gets bored when nobody does.

Touch it

Go on, pet it.

Move your cursor or finger back and forth across it to stroke. Press and hold to push hard. Same rules as the pressure matrix inside it.

Pressure
Mood: wandering

The real thing: gentle pressure across the whole velostat sheet → heart eyes and it stops; any point over 90% → angry, runs off for 2 seconds.

Photos: NYU DocLab

Behavior

Its whole temper, on one page.

By default it roams inside a set area. At the edge it turns 80–100°. A light touch: it stops, gets happy, wiggles its ears. A hard press: it gets angry and runs. Three minutes alone: bored. Click to zoom.

WanderFour photoresistors underneath watch for a black line, so it stays on its own turf like a pet.
HappyGentle strokes for over a second: heart eyes, it stops, ears wiggle.
AngryToo hard: furrowed brow, an anger mark, then it bolts.
Build

A tiny body that had to fit everything.

We modeled the shell in Blender and 3D printed it, with cutouts for the screen, ear servos and sensors. The hard part was space: wheels, motors, two Nanos and a fistful of wire all had to fit.

Hardware

Two Nanos: one feels, one moves.

Feel: one Nano scans a velostat pressure matrix, picks the mood and draws it on an ST7789 screen.

Move: it sends a one-letter command (go / stop / normal) over serial to the second Nano, which drives the wheels and reads the photoresistors to stay in bounds.

For a company salon later, we added two servo ears on a PCA9685 board, so a touch moves the ears too. In the end the whole robot ran fully wireless.

Reflection

Touch is the fastest way to build a bond.

Shape is the manualNobody needed instructions. People read the shape and knew whether to tap or stroke.
Space is harder than codeH-bridges, photoresistors, power and so much wire. We shorted it on the ITP floor more than once.
A little temper goes a long wayGetting angry or bored made people want to win it over.
What’s next

Let it remember you: turn touch history into a lasting bond instead of starting fresh every time.

It’s also where my thesis direction, Tangible AI, began.
